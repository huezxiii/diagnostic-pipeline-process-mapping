01 · The problem

17.9 hours of every result was waiting, not testing

Queues, manual re-keying, batch courier runs and paper exception handling across five departments made up 96% of the cycle. That cost the network in three places.

$4.8M

ED congestion. Emergency length of stay grew 3.4 hours per patient while urgent panels waited, triggering penalties, bed blockage and walk-outs.

14.2%

Rework. Share of daily orders needing manual intervention or re-collection: label mismatches, missing clinical data, degraded samples.

$1.2M

Overtime and freight. Unbudgeted annual spend as uncoordinated batch arrivals forced the central lab into continuous emergency overtime.

03 · As-Is process map

Five lanes, 18 steps, four queues

The current pipeline crosses 5 functional lanes with 18 mapped steps, 9 departmental handoffs and 4 manual approval checkpoints. Queue times are mined means from the event log.

04 · Root causes

Four bottlenecks did most of the damage

Event-log mining ranked every stage by non-value-added minutes. Central accessioning alone held a quarter of the waste.

1. Central accessioning (Queue 3)

4.5 h mean queue

Mechanism
60% of clinic specimens arrive in one wave between 4 and 6 PM. Staff rescan every tube and cross-check IDs in a legacy terminal while evening staffing drops 30%.
Impact
A 4.5-hour backlog and an 8.4% re-key error rate.

2. STAT priority inversion

38% of orders tagged STAT

Mechanism
Clinicians tag routine work as STAT to jump slow queues, flooding the express centrifuges.
Impact
True ED emergencies wait 152 min in the testing queue, longer than tagged-but-routine STATs at 119 min.

3. Fixed courier schedules

2.8 h in holding bins

Mechanism
Couriers run at 9, 12 and 3 regardless of whether a clinic has 2 tubes or 200 waiting.
Impact
Room-temperature storage drives a 3.2% hemolysis and rejection rate.

4. Paper exception tickets

18.0 h to resolve

Mechanism
Unreadable barcodes or demographic mismatches generate paper rejection tickets routed by inter-department mail.
Impact
Samples expire while tickets sit for 12 to 24 hours, forcing re-draws.

06 · To-Be process map

12 steps, automated handoffs

The redesign removes manual re-keying and batch logistics. Routing is dynamic and exceptions resolve digitally.

07 · Re-engineering levers

Four changes, each aimed at one queue

Queue 3 · Accessioning

Optical accessioning sorters

Robotic sorters scan, uncap and centrifuge 1,200 tubes an hour with no manual re-keying.

4.5 h → 12 min

Queue 1 · Clinic hold

Volume-triggered dispatch

When 25 tubes are scanned into a smart box, the nearest courier is dispatched through a logistics API.

2.8 h → 35 min

Queue 4 · Priority

STAT governance in the EHR

STAT needs an objective trigger such as active ED status or acute vitals. Overrides need medical-director approval.

38% STAT → 8% STAT

Exceptions

Digital exception portal

Label mismatches push a notification to the drawing clinic's tablet for photo verification instead of a paper ticket.

18 h → 22 min

11 · Takeaway

Throughput is a flow problem before it is a hardware problem

The analyzer was never the constraint. Mapping the full pipeline, measuring every queue from event timestamps and governing order priority turned a $6.5M hardware disappointment into a 75% faster service and $4.35M a year in recovered value.
